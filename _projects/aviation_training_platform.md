### Overview

I work as a Senior Software Engineer at [AVIAR](https://www.aviar.nl/), an
Amsterdam company founded in 2019 that builds VR training for the aviation
industry. The team combines XR developers with aviation experts, and the goal
is to make VR a standard way to train staff in air transportation.

Everything below comes from the company's public website. Product internals

## What the platform does

According to [aviar.nl](https://www.aviar.nl/), the platform includes:

- **VR Training Hub** — a library of training modules that runs on standalone VR headsets.
- **Training Station Software** — lets a trainer prepare, run and monitor sessions on several headsets from a PC, laptop or tablet on the local network, without an internet connection.
- **Driver Training** — driving accessories connect directly to the headset, so no PC is needed.
- **All-In-One VR Training Box** — hardware, software, accounts and device management delivered as a subscription, with remote updates and trainer onboarding.
- **Custom VR Training** — modules built to a client's own procedures.

## Training areas

The website lists [ground operations and cabin crew training](https://www.aviar.nl/#section-areas),
with MRO and airport security training announced as coming soon. Ground
operations modules include pre-arrival checks, aircraft marshalling, GSE
connection, pre-departure checks, cargo door operations and pushback with a
towbar.

## Clients

Public clients shown on [the website](https://www.aviar.nl/#section-clients)

Schiphol, Cebu Pacific, Asiana Airlines and Celebi.
